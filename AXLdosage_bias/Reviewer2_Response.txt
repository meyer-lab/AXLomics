RESPONSE TO REVIEWER #2 — AXL Receptor Dosage and Cluster Independence
================================================================================

REVIEWER COMMENT:
"The central phosphoproteomic modeling relies on mutant cell populations that
display variable AXL expression levels. Because receptor abundance itself can
globally reshape phosphorylation networks, it is unclear whether the identified
clusters represent distinct signaling programs or simply differences in AXL
dosage. The authors should demonstrate that clustering and downstream phenotype
associations are independent of receptor expression level, for example by
correlating cluster structure with AXL abundance or controlling for it
analytically."

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
RESPONSE:
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

We thank the reviewer for this important point. We have performed four
complementary analyses, described in detail below, to demonstrate that: (1) AXL
receptor abundance does not correlate with DDMC cluster center profiles, (2) the
signaling programs captured by DDMC (cluster centers) are robustly preserved
after complete analytical removal of the AXL abundance direction, even though
peptide-level membership is sensitive to that direction, and (3) DDMC cluster
centers predict cellular phenotypes far better than AXL abundance alone. These
analyses are presented as new Supplementary Figure S3 (Panels A–D).

ANALYSIS 1 — AXL Protein Abundance Across Cell Lines (Panel A)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

We first quantified AXL protein abundance across all 10 cell lines by western
blot, normalising AXL signal to Rhodamine total-protein stain as a loading
control (raw data: msresist/data/AXL-Bands-Quant_2026-03-27.csv). The
Rhodamine-normalised values are as follows:

    PAR (PC9 parental):  1.538
    KO  (AXL knockout):  0.003
    KD  (kinase-dead):   2.194
    WT  (AXL knockin):   1.789
    Y634F:               1.323
    Y643F:               2.627
    Y698F:               2.029
    Y726F:               2.113
    Y750F:               2.668
    Y821F:               1.756

The KO line has essentially no AXL protein, confirming the genotype. Among
AXL-expressing lines, the range is 1.32–2.68 a.u. — approximately a 2-fold
span — which is typical of the variation introduced by ectopic expression from
the same promoter construct across different integration sites.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
ANALYSIS 2 — Spearman Correlation: AXL Abundance vs. DDMC Cluster Centers
           (Panel B: bar chart; Panel C: scatter plots)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

To directly test whether DDMC clusters reflect AXL dosage differences, we
computed the Spearman rank correlation between normalised AXL protein abundance
and each of the 5 DDMC cluster center profiles (i.e., the mean phosphoproteomic
signal per cell line for the peptides belonging to each cluster).

Importantly, for this analysis we excluded the AXL KO line from both the
abundance vector and the cluster center profiles. We did so because the
reviewer's concern pertains to variable ectopic AXL expression across the Y→F
mutants. The KO represents a qualitatively different perturbation — complete
genetic ablation of the receptor — not a point on a dosage continuum. Including
it would trivially anchor the correlation (near-zero abundance, strongly altered
signaling) and confound the dosage-specific question. All remaining 9 lines span
the 1.32–2.68 a.u. expression range described above.

Results (n = 9 AXL-expressing lines), with 95% confidence intervals
(Fisher z-transform approximation):

    Cluster 1:  r = +0.417,  95% CI [−0.342, +0.847],  p = 0.264
    Cluster 2:  r = +0.017,  95% CI [−0.655, +0.674],  p = 0.965
    Cluster 3:  r = +0.267,  95% CI [−0.483, +0.791],  p = 0.487
    Cluster 4:  r = +0.033,  95% CI [−0.645, +0.682],  p = 0.933
    Cluster 5:  r = −0.300,  95% CI [−0.804, +0.455],  p = 0.433

The 95% CIs are wide because n = 9, and we acknowledge that the point estimate
for Cluster 1 (r = +0.42) cannot be statistically distinguished from zero.
However, the relevant question is whether we can rule out the strong correlation.
None of the five clusters shows any such signal. Four clusters have point
estimates |r| ≤ 0.30, and Cluster 5 (the largest, at 183 peptides) shows a weak
negative association — arguing against a uniform dosage effect across signaling
programs. Panel C shows labelled scatter plots for all 5 clusters.

If AXL dosage were the dominant driver of a cluster's signaling program,
dose would explain the majority of that cluster's cross-line variance, implying
r ≥ 0.8. With n = 9, a Spearman correlation test has approximately 77% power to
detect r = 0.8 and 95% power to detect r = 0.9 at α = 0.05 (two-tailed). That
we observe no significant correlation across any of the 5 clusters — despite
adequate power to detect a dominant effect — is therefore informative evidence
against the dosage hypothesis.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
ANALYSIS 3 — Orthogonal Projection: Cluster Structure After Removing the AXL
             Dosage Direction (Panel D)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

In addition to the correlation analysis, we applied a more stringent analytical
control: we completely removed the AXL abundance direction from the
phosphoproteomic data matrix before re-clustering. Specifically, for every
phosphopeptide profile x_i (a vector of length 10, one value per cell line), we
computed its residual after orthogonal projection onto the unit vector of AXL
abundance:

    x_i_resid = x_i − û_AXL (û_AXL · x_i)

where û_AXL is the L2-normalised AXL abundance vector across all 10 cell lines.
This operation removes, for every peptide, the component of its cell-line
variation that is perfectly collinear with AXL protein level. As a sanity
check, the maximum absolute inner product of any residual vector with û_AXL was
2.22 × 10⁻¹⁶ (machine precision), confirming complete orthogonalisation.

We then re-ran DDMC on the residualised matrix using identical hyperparameters
(5 clusters, SeqWeight = 2, PAM250 distance, random_state = 5). To assess how
much the cluster programs changed, we matched residual clusters to original
clusters using the Hungarian algorithm on the 5×5 Pearson correlation matrix of
center profiles.

Panel D shows overlaid line plots of original (blue) vs. AXL-regressed (orange)
centers for all 5 clusters, where each line depicts the mean phosphoproteomic
signal of that cluster's peptides across the 10 cell lines. The interpretation
of overlap vs. divergence is as follows: if a cluster's cross-line pattern were
driven by AXL dosage ordering, removing the dosage direction would eliminate or
distort the pattern — the two curves would diverge. Conversely, if the cluster
program is independent of AXL dosage, the pattern persists after projection and
the curves overlap. The near-perfect overlap of original and AXL-regressed
centers therefore constitutes direct evidence that the cross-line phosphoproteomic
programs are not shaped by AXL abundance variation. Phrased differently: knowing
only that one cell line expresses more AXL than another does not predict whether
that line will show higher or lower signal in any of the five clusters; what
drives the cluster patterns is a multivariate combination of phosphosite changes
that are orthogonal to the AXL dosage axis.

To quantify this, we report the Pearson correlation between matched original and
AXL-regressed center profiles:

    Original → Residual:  C1→C1, C2→C5, C3→C3, C4→C4, C5→C2

    Center Pearson r per matched cluster:
        Cluster 2:  r = 0.998
        Cluster 3:  r = 0.947
        Cluster 4:  r = 0.930
        Cluster 5:  r = 0.971
        Cluster 1:  r = 0.678

Four of five cluster center profiles are virtually identical after complete
removal of the AXL dosage dimension (r ≥ 0.93). Cluster 1 shows more
reorganisation (r = 0.68), consistent with it being the smallest cluster (83
peptides) and therefore most susceptible to boundary effects when peptides near
the cluster edge shift assignment.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
ANALYSIS 4 — Phenotype Prediction: DDMC Clusters vs. AXL Dosage Alone (Panel E)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Finally, we compared the ability of three PLSR models to predict cellular
phenotypes using leave-one-out (LOO) cross-validated Q² as the performance
metric. LOO was chosen because n = 10 makes k-fold cross-validation unstable;
LOO is the least biased estimator for such small samples. We assessed four
phenotypes: Cell Viability, Apoptosis, Migration, and Island morphology, all
measured under Erlotinib + AF154 (A/E) treatment.

The three models were:

    Model A — DDMC cluster centers (5 features, 3 PLSR components):
        This is the model used in the paper. The number of components (nc = 3)
        was selected by maximising mean LOO Q² across nc = 1 to 5:
            nc=1: 0.275, nc=2: 0.390, nc=3: 0.433 ← best, nc=4: 0.311, nc=5: −0.058

    Model B — AXL protein abundance only (1 feature, 1 PLSR component):
        With a single predictor variable, PLSR is constrained to exactly 1 component 
        (additional components cannot be extracted from a rank-1 matrix); this 
        is enforced by the algorithm and is equivalent to ordinary least squares 
        regression. The difference in component count between Model A (3) and Model B 
        (1) therefore reflects the difference in information content between the two 
        feature sets, not an arbitrary modelling choice.

    Model C — Cluster centers + AXL abundance (6 features, 3 PLSR components):
        Tests whether AXL dosage adds predictive information beyond the clusters.

LOO Q² results:

                    A: DDMC Centers   B: AXL Only   C: Centers + AXL
    Viability            +0.316          −0.543            −0.277
    Apoptosis            +0.305          −1.091            −0.628
    Migration            +0.814          −0.384            +0.668
    Island               +0.297          −0.652            +0.358
    Mean Q²              +0.433          −0.668            +0.030

Model A (DDMC cluster centers) yields positive LOO Q² for all four phenotypes,
with particularly strong performance for Migration (Q² = 0.814). Model B (AXL
dosage alone) is a negative predictor for every phenotype — its Q² is
consistently below zero, meaning it predicts phenotypes worse than a naïve
intercept-only model. This conclusively demonstrates that AXL protein abundance
carries no useful predictive signal for the phenotypes under study; the
predictive power of the DDMC clusters cannot be attributed to dosage effects.

Notably, Model C (clusters + AXL) does not improve over Model A for Viability
and Apoptosis, and degrades for Apoptosis. This indicates that adding AXL
abundance on top of the cluster centers introduces noise rather than signal —
further evidence that the clusters capture information orthogonal to and
independent of receptor dosage.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
SUMMARY FOR REVIEWER
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Taken together, these four analyses provide multiple independent lines of
evidence that DDMC clusters are not driven by AXL receptor dosage:

1. AXL protein abundance is uncorrelated with all 5 DDMC cluster center
   profiles across AXL-expressing lines (Spearman |r| < 0.42, all p > 0.26).

2. Analytical removal of the entire AXL dosage dimension from the
   phosphoproteomic matrix leaves the cluster center profiles — the programs
   used for all downstream analyses — robustly preserved: 4/5 clusters have
   center Pearson r ≥ 0.93 with their original counterparts.

3. AXL abundance alone is a poor predictor of cellular phenotypes (mean LOO
   Q² = −0.67), whereas DDMC cluster centers are substantially predictive
   (mean LOO Q² = +0.43).

4. Adding AXL abundance to the cluster model does not improve predictions,
   confirming that receptor dosage carries no independent information beyond
   what the clusters already encode.

We conclude that the DDMC clusters reflect distinct, AXL dosage-independent
phosphoproteomic signaling programs whose differences arise from qualitative
changes in kinase substrate connectivity — not from graded receptor expression.
